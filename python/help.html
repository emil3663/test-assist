Test Assist

Annotation & capture tool — Help Guide

Version 1.4.0

Overview

Test Assist is a lightweight always-on-top capture and annotation utility designed for testers and developers. It lets you screenshot or record your screen, annotate the result with shapes, text, and drawings, then copy or save the output instantly.

The interface has two parts: the Floating Launcher (the small overlay panel) and the Editor (the full annotation workspace).

Floating Launcher

Floating Launcher — labelled diagram

  • 1 Editor, updates, shrink to strip, hide to tray. Drag the header to move the panel
  • 2 Capture Region — draw a selection
  • 3 Full Screen, and the round red Record button
  • 4 Recent — your last three captures
  • 5 Open / raise the Editor window
  • 6 Shortcuts, and where the close button puts things
Moving and shrinking the launcher
Drag anywhere on the panel (outside a button) to reposition it freely. Drag it toward the right edge of your screen and it docks automatically as a compact vertical strip; the — button in the header does the same thing deliberately. The expand icon at the bottom of the strip brings the full panel back.

The ✕ button does not quit — it hides Test Assist to the system tray, and the tray icon brings it back. To quit, right-click anywhere on the panel.
Capturing Alt+P
Each action is its own button — there is no mode to set first.

Capture Region (Alt+P) dims the screen so you can drag a selection over the area you want. Release the mouse and the screenshot goes straight to the Editor. A selection may span two monitors; it is composited from both rather than clipped to one.

Full Screen (Alt+Shift+P) captures the whole screen you are working on, including the taskbar and clock.
Recording Alt+V
The round red button starts a full-screen recording. While it runs, the panel makes that obvious rather than leaving you to find the stop control: the capture buttons give way to a single Stop Recording button, a red dot and a running clock appear, and the panel takes a red border. In the docked strip the same round button becomes a square stop in place, with the elapsed time beneath it.

Click Stop Recording (or the round button, or Alt+V) to finish — the recording is assembled into a single .mp4 and saved to Documents\Test Assist\. An Open folder button appears once it's done, so finding the file doesn't mean remembering where it went.

If MP4 assembly is ever unavailable, nothing is lost: the captured frames are kept on disk instead of the video.

Recordings are capped at three minutes, and frames are scaled to 1280px wide — a repro that needs longer or full resolution should be split into more than one recording.

The docked strip
Click — in the header, or drag the panel to the right edge of the screen, to collapse it to a slim vertical strip. It carries the same three capture actions in the same order as the full panel — it is the panel with the labels removed, not a different tool.

This is the mode worth leaving on screen for a long test session, which is why recording is designed around it: the round red button becomes a square stop in place, so the control you pressed to start is the one you press to finish, and the elapsed time appears directly beneath it.
Check for Updates
Click the update icon in the header row to check whether a newer version is available. This is manual only — nothing is checked automatically, and nothing contacts GitHub unless you click it. If a newer version exists, the dialog links to its release page; to actually install it, close Test Assist, download the new zip, and replace the contents of the folder you run it from — the app is installed by unzipping, so that's the whole update.
Multiple monitors — region capture, full-screen capture, and recording all follow whichever screen you're actually using or working on, not always the main display. A selection that spans two screens is captured from both, side by side.

Editor

The Editor opens automatically after every capture. It contains a toolbar of annotation tools across the top, the canvas in the centre, and a controls panel on the right.

With the Editor open and nothing loaded, use the 📂 Open Image button in the toolbar to bring in an existing file, or press Ctrl+V to paste an image from the clipboard. Both use the same validation a capture does — a bad path or an empty clipboard leaves the Editor exactly as it was, with a message, rather than failing silently.

About & bug reports ⓘ
Click the ⓘ button beside Help to open the About dialog. It shows the running version and your operating system version, and its Copy details for a bug report button copies all of that plus your full display layout — screen count, each screen's geometry, and its scaling (device pixel ratio) — to the clipboard in one click. Pasting this into a bug report is the fastest way to get a defect diagnosed, especially anything to do with multiple monitors or mixed scaling.

Annotation Tools

S — Select

Click a shape's border to select it — eight handles appear. Drag the border to move it, a corner to resize both axes, or an edge handle to change width or height alone. Interiors are click-through, so a big rectangle never blocks the marks inside it.

Hold Shift to keep the proportions while resizing, or to lock a move to one axis. Arrow keys nudge by a pixel, Shift+arrow by ten. Esc abandons a drag in progress and Delete removes the selection. Everything here is undoable. The cursor tells you what a press will do before you press it.

X — Crop

Drag a rectangle to crop the canvas to that region. The original image outside the selection is discarded. Undo (Ctrl+Z) restores the original.

B — Blur

Drag to draw a blur region. Useful for redacting sensitive information. Adjust strength with the Stroke slider.

T — Text

Click anywhere to place a text box. Type your label, then click outside or press Escape to finish. Drag the corner handle to resize the text box.

With Text selected, the settings bar changes to match: the size slider reads pt and sets the font size, and the opacity slider sets the background behind the text rather than a highlight fill. A second swatch appears for the background colour.

Text sits on a dark plate by default, because a screenshot is busy and a red note over a red error banner is otherwise unreadable. Set the opacity to 0 for bare text over a plain area.

H — Highlight

Drag to draw a semi-transparent filled rectangle. Adjust fill opacity using the Highlight Fill slider in the panel.

C — Circle

Drag to draw an ellipse. Pick a colour using the colour swatch below the button.

A — Arrow

Drag from the tail to the head of the arrow. Choose Classic, Double, or Dashed style from the Arrow combo box in the panel.

R — Rectangle

Drag to draw an outline rectangle. Great for callout boxes.

P — Pen

Freehand drawing. Hold and drag to sketch paths. Stroke size is controlled by the Stroke slider.

Colour swatches — Each colour-capable tool has a small colour swatch below its button. Click the swatch to open a colour picker and choose a custom colour. Colours are remembered per tool.

Controls Panel (right dock)

Edit section

  • Undo / Redo — step through annotation history.
  • Delete Selected — remove the currently selected annotation.
  • Bring to Front / Send Backward / Send to Back — reorder the layers of the selected annotation.
  • Clear All — remove every annotation (a confirmation dialog appears).

Zoom

Use + / − buttons or the slider to zoom in/out. Click Fit to automatically zoom so the entire image fills the viewport.

Stroke

Controls the line width for most tools (1–20 px).

Arrow style

Switches between Classic, Double, and Dashed arrow styles. Applies to the next arrow drawn and also updates any currently selected arrow.

Highlight Fill

Controls the opacity of the Highlight tool fill (0–100%). Lower values are more transparent; 100% is fully opaque.

Saving & Exporting

💾 Save PNG Ctrl+S
Composites the base image and all annotations into a single PNG and opens a save dialog. The result is also added to the History sidebar automatically.
⧉ Copy
Copies the composited annotated image to the system clipboard so you can paste it directly into Jira, Slack, email, or any other tool.
📋 Export JSON
Saves the raw annotation data as a JSON file. Useful for re-importing annotations or processing them programmatically.

History

Every capture — region or full-screen — is added to the History sidebar the moment it's taken, so nothing is lost if you take another capture before saving the first. Saving or copying an image also adds a snapshot. Recordings appear there too, shown as a distinct video card rather than a thumbnail — clicking one opens it in your video player instead of loading it into the canvas.

Use the Show dropdown to filter: Recent 5, Today, This Week, or This Month. Click the HISTORY header to open a full gallery overlay with all category tabs. Clicking a snapshot loads it back into the editor; clicking a recording opens it externally.

Menus

File, Edit, Window, Help
Everything in the toolbars is also reachable from the menu bar, which follows each platform's own convention.
  • File — Open Image, Save PNG, Export JSON, Quit
  • Edit — Undo, Redo, Delete Selected, the layering commands, and Clear All (which still asks before it clears)
  • Window — Show Launcher, Show Editor
  • Help — this page, Check for Updates, About Test Assist
On macOS, About and Quit appear in the Test Assist menu beside the Apple logo, where a Mac user expects them, rather than under Help and File. On Windows they stay under Help and File, where a Windows user expects them.

Appearance

Light and dark
Test Assist follows whatever your operating system is set to — there is no setting of its own to keep in step. This help page does the same, which is why it matches the app you opened it from.

Changing your system theme while Test Assist is running needs a restart to take effect.

Annotations never change with the theme. They are drawn on top of your screenshot rather than on the app's own background, so the same defect marked up on a light machine and a dark one exports identical evidence — your appearance setting should not be visible in a bug report.
Windows, macOS and Linux
Windows is the primary target and the only one with a packaged installer. macOS runs from source and can be built into a Test Assist.app; Linux runs from source.

Two macOS notes. A build that is not signed by an Apple Developer account is blocked by Gatekeeper on first launch — right-click the app and choose Open, then Open again. And screen capture needs permission under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording; without it macOS returns black images rather than reporting an error.

Global shortcuts (Alt+P, Alt+Shift+P, Alt+V) work on Windows and macOS. On Linux they are not registered, and the launcher stops advertising them rather than claiming a shortcut that does nothing.

Tips & Tricks

  • The launcher stays on top of all windows so you can capture at any time without alt-tabbing away.
  • After capturing, the Editor opens in the background — your flow isn't interrupted. Click the TA button in the launcher (or dock strip) to bring it to the front.
  • Use Crop first to trim unwanted areas, then annotate — the crop is fully undoable.
  • Text: with the Select tool, click inside a placed label to change its words, or drag its border to move it.
  • History snapshots that can no longer be decoded as an image are cleaned up on startup. Small but valid captures are kept.
